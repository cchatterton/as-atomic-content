Atomic Design — what is it?

Atomic design is a way to understand and create an interface as both a whole and a set of connected parts. Brad Frost describes five levels that help a team move between a small element, a working component, and the page a person actually uses. It is a way of thinking about an interface, not a prescribed set of WordPress content types.

The five levels

  1. Atoms are the smallest useful interface elements, such as a label, input, or button.
  2. Molecules combine elements into a simple working unit, such as a labelled search field with its button.
  3. Organisms bring smaller units together into a distinct section, such as a site header.
  4. Templates arrange sections into a page layout and define the structure that content needs, before the final content is added.
  5. Pages fill a template with real content. They show how the complete interface works and reveal where the smaller parts need to improve.

Work between the parts and the whole

These levels are not five steps that must be finished in order. A page with real content can expose a problem in a component; improving that component can then improve other pages that use it. Atomic design gives the team a shared way to discuss those relationships and make deliberate decisions about reuse.

How we apply the idea here

We organise composable content with Cards, Rows, and WordPress Patterns. Those are tools we chose to put the idea into practice on this site. They are not the five levels of atomic design, and a Card, Row, or Pattern does not automatically correspond to one level. The other guides explain how each tool stores content and how changes reach its placements.
